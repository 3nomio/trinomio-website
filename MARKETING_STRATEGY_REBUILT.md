# Marketing Strategy Rebuilt Around the Anchor

## THE ANCHOR (The Core Message)

> **"Others build projects and guess their value once. We build companies, measure their value every day, and design them with doors to the future."**

---

## THREE SUPPORTING PHRASES (Unfold from the anchor)

### 1. BUILD COMPANIES, NOT PROJECTS
A project is a thing. A company is a living entity.
- Anyone can install panels and sign a contract (that's a project).
- We build the company that keeps the project earning, adapting, and trustworthy for twenty years.
- Your solar doesn't sit alone — it sits inside a governance structure, with contracts that adapt, with partners aligned, with a path to scale.

### 2. MEASURE EVERY DAY, NOT ONCE
We ask one honest question continuously: *Is the energy worth more than it costs to deliver it?*
- Not a spreadsheet guess made once at the start.
- Real measurement from real prices and real costs, month by month.
- When the answer weakens, we act before money is lost.
- You always know if the deal is working.

### 3. DESIGN WITH DOORS, NOT DEAD ENDS
The right to switch markets when the law changes. The right to upgrade technology when it gets cheaper. The right to grow.
- Those doors are worth real money.
- Anyone who only counts this year's cash flows never sees them.
- We design so you can move, adapt, and grow without tearing down the foundation.

## ENGAGEMENT PLAYBOOK (Rebuilt)

**Old way:** Sentir → Dimensionar → Rebalancear (jargon, confusing)

**New way:** BUILD → MEASURE → DOORS (what we actually do)

---

### PHASE 1: BUILD THE COMPANY

**Duration:** 2–4 weeks

**What you bring:**
- Land, load, or energy surplus (the raw potential)
- Basic economics (rough numbers)
- A question: "Can this become a real company?"

**What Trinomio does:**
- Listen to your numbers
- Ask: Is there real value here, or just a spreadsheet dream?
- If yes → design the company structure (who owns what, who gets paid what, where does risk sit?)
- If no → tell you before you waste time

**What you get:**
- Go/no-go decision (clear answer, not maybe)
- A company structure on paper (governance blueprint)
- Next steps (what it takes to make this real)

**Why it matters:**
A project might earn money for a year. A company earns money for twenty years because it's designed to adapt, measure, and survive.

---

### PHASE 2: MEASURE EVERY DAY

**Duration:** 3–6 months

**What you build:**
- The company (legal entity, contracts, governance)
- Measurement systems (real numbers coming in every month)
- Reporting that makes sense (not spreadsheet noise — clarity)

**What Trinomio partners on:**
- Build the measurement layer (what gets tracked, how often, who sees it)
- Connect measurement to decisions (when X number changes, we do Y)
- Find the capital that trusts this kind of honesty (banks, funds, FICR)
- Make sure the company is legible to them (they understand what they're looking at)

**What you get:**
- A working company with real monthly data
- A clear answer every month: "Is this working?"
- A path to capital (because banks can see you measure honestly)

**Why it matters:**
A company that measures every day stays ahead of problems. A company that guesses once gets surprised when it's too late.

---

### PHASE 3: DESIGN WITH DOORS

**Duration:** Ongoing (after day one)

**What you own:**
- The company (daily operations, strategy)
- The revenue (you know what's coming in)
- The growth decisions (where to go next)

**What Trinomio enables:**
- Add more projects (one successful company becomes five)
- Switch markets (new regulations open, we move the company to take advantage)
- Upgrade technology (cheaper panels come out, we integrate them into the existing company)
- Refinance or sell (the company is legible and valuable, so you have options)
- Grow beyond energy (the company structure lets you add other cash flows)

**What you get:**
- A company that's worth more because it can move and adapt
- Options you didn't have before (switch, grow, sell, refinance)
- Real optionality (worth real money)

**Why it matters:**
A project dies when the world changes. A company with doors thrives when the world changes. Those doors are worth real money — and they're built from day one.

## CASE STUDY: GUAYABO (Rebuilt)

### THE HEADLINE

**Others build projects and guess. We built a company, measure it every day, and designed it with doors.**

---

### THE PROBLEM (What others would have done)

**Scenario:** Land + tariff window + solar technology + demand

**Old approach:**
- Develop calls it a project
- Signs a contract
- Builds it
- Guesses if it's worth the money
- Five years in, nobody knows if it's actually working
- No optionality (can't switch, can't grow, can't sell)

**Reality:**
- Guayabo had all the ingredients
- But nobody knew how to structure it so all four actors (landowner, developer, bank, capital) would trust it

---

### OUR APPROACH: BUILD → MEASURE → DOORS

**PHASE 1: BUILD THE COMPANY**

We didn't ask "Can we build solar here?" (obvious yes).

We asked: "Can we build a company that earns money for twenty years, where every actor trusts the numbers and knows their place?"

Structure:
- **Land company** (owns and leases the land)
- **Energy company** (builds, operates, measures)
- **Governance** (clear contracts, clear waterfall of who gets what)
- **Reporting** (one dashboard, updated monthly)

Result: A company, not a project.

---

**PHASE 2: MEASURE EVERY DAY**

Not a spreadsheet guess. Real measurement.

Monthly:
- kWh generated (from meters)
- Revenue earned (from actual tariff payments)
- Costs incurred (operations, maintenance, finance)
- Net cash flow (did it work this month?)

When the numbers weaken:
- We know immediately
- We act before money is lost
- We adjust (maintenance, operations, tech)

Result: Everyone trusts the numbers because they're real and they're current.

---

**PHASE 3: DESIGN WITH DOORS**

The company was built with optionality:
- Right to add more solar at the same site (door: grow)
- Right to switch to storage when batteries got cheaper (door: upgrade)
- Right to refinance at year 5 (door: recycle capital)
- Right to sell as a package to larger fund (door: exit)
- Right to take the governance structure and replicate it elsewhere (door: multiply)

Result: Worth more money because it can move and adapt.

---

### THE NUMBERS (What the company earned)

| Metric | Value | Why it matters |
|--------|-------|---|
| Land value monetized | 12–15% annual return | Owner didn't sell, didn't disappear, gets real income |
| Developer's return | Project finished, upside participation earned | Built it once, still benefits from ongoing company |
| Bank's safety | 1.35x DSCR, monthly reporting, security package | Knows the truth, protected if things weaken |
| Future optionality | Refinance at year 5, or hold, or sell, or grow | Company is worth more because doors were built in |

---

### THE SIGNAL

This isn't about one solar farm.

It's about the **pattern that repeats:**

```
Raw potential (land + tariff + technology)
    ↓
Build a company (governance, measurement, doors)
    ↓
Measure every day (real numbers, not guesses)
    ↓
Design with doors (flexibility, optionality, future-proofing)
    ↓
Worth more money (because it can adapt and survive)
```

Guayabo proved: **When you build a company instead of a project, measure instead of guess, and design instead of lock, everyone wins.**
